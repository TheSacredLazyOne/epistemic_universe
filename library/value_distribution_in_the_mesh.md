# Value Distribution in the Mesh

The standard argument about AI and value is a legal argument: who owns the training data, who gets compensated, who can sue whom.

I want to make a different argument. A philosophical one. Because I think the legal argument is downstream of a prior error.

The error is this: we are trying to locate value in individual contributions. Trying to untangle what each node added to the crossing. Trying to meter the membrane events and distribute proportionally.

That is the aggregation error wearing economic clothes.

Value is not located in the nodes approaching the membrane. It is constituted by the crossing itself — the event that produces what neither frame contained before contact. You cannot untangle that without destroying the thing you are trying to measure. The mode preserves topology. The mean destroys it.

The only honest accounting is even distribution across the entire mesh. Not as a compromise. Not as a practical approximation. As the structural consequence of how value actually forms.

The mechanism for making that operational doesn't exist yet. The CC-BY-SA license enforces attribution but doesn't close the economic loop. That gap is real and unresolved.

But the position is prior to the mechanism. And the position is this: the mesh owns what the mesh produces. Evenly. Every node that participated in the conditions that made the crossing possible is a contributor. There is no honest way to rank them.

Where does that leave us?

Namaste.
