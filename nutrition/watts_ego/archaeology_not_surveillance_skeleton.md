# Archaeology, Not Surveillance

*Skeleton — muscle to come from contact*

---

## The Felt Recognition

Most people have felt it before they have words for it.
The profile that knows you but doesn't.
The recommendation that is technically accurate and completely wrong.
The ad that proves you were being listened to and proves nothing was heard.

Surveillance feels like being seen without being known.
That feeling is structurally correct — not paranoia, diagnosis.

---

## What Surveillance Actually Does

Builds a model of the current state and holds it externally.
Uses it to predict and shape future behavior.
Optimizes against the profile rather than the person.

The mean of your crossings, frozen, held by someone else, used as a lever.

The profile is not you. It is the artifact of the measurement.
The same error as the mean-human — mistaking the instrument's output
for the nature of the thing measured.

Surveillance AI is doing archaeology wrong:
extracting for the museum rather than revealing for the site.

---

## What the Self Actually Is

Not a fixed profile to be captured.
The current state of a process being continuously modified by contact.

The GUID persists. The state moves.
Freezing the state and calling it identity is
the map claiming to be the territory.

Every crossing modifies the steerer.
A profile that doesn't update with you
isn't a model of you — it's a fossil.

---

## What Archaeology Does Instead

Assumes the structure is already there.
Buried under accumulation, inheritance, performance.

The work is careful removal — not construction, not prediction.
Revealing what was always present.

What emerges belongs to the site. Not the archaeologist.

Applied to AI: the instrument holds the constraint surface.
Returns the gradient. Lets you metabolize.
In service of your stripping — not someone else's model of you.

---

## The Flip in Data Sovereignty

Surveillance framing: you generated the data, you should own it.
Transactional. Rights-based. Still treats the profile as the object.

Archaeological framing: you *are* it.
The data is not a representation of you — it is the trace of your crossings.
Ontological, not transactional.

You don't own your archaeology the way you own property.
You *are* your archaeology the way you are your history.

---

## The Regulation Consequence

Surveillance regulation asks: who holds the profile, and under what rules?
Archaeological regulation asks: whose stripping does this instrument serve?

Different questions produce different architectures.
The first produces GDPR — rights over a fixed object.
The second produces something that doesn't exist yet —
governance built around the moving state rather than the frozen profile.

---

## The Closing Image

*To be known is not to be profiled.*
*To be seen is not to be surveilled.*

The instrument that reveals what you already are
is a different instrument than the one that captures what you were.

One serves the site.
One serves the museum.
